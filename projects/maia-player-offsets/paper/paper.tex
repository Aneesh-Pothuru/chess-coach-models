\begin{abstract}
The app adds \Base{} points before it converts a chess.com rating to a
\ModelName{} band. The predecessor kept that offset, but its exploratory
results differed by side and rating. This phase fixes a comparison of
separate offsets for the player and his opponents, and a single offset
function of rating. The archive contributes \Moves{} moves in \Games{}
games. We rebuilt the input and timed the app's instruments. No candidate
has been fitted or scored. The projected run takes \FullHours{} hours with
\Processes{} workers; we allow \PlanningHours{} hours for sustained load.
\end{abstract}

\section{Candidates and estimation}
Candidate A jointly fits separate player and opponent offsets over
\OffsetMin{} to \OffsetMax{} in steps of \OffsetStep{} (\ACount{}
combinations). Each person's offset follows that person into either input
of the model. Candidate B uses the app's clamping and rounding on
$a+b(r-\Anchor)$, with the same intercept grid and slopes
\SlopeList{} (\BCount{} combinations). A line is the smallest family that
can follow a rating trend; both slope directions are allowed. Each family
contains the baseline. Every band is computed by the app's conversion.

Both fits maximize mean natural log probability per fitting move. The
held-out gain is candidate minus baseline mean log probability. We use
\Draws{} shared bootstrap draws of held-out games with replacement, seed
\Seed, taking the ratio of summed differences to move counts. Fits stay
fixed. Exact fitting ties prefer closeness to the baseline for A and a
smaller absolute slope for B; the README specifies every further tie-break
and quantile index.

\section{The fixed decision}
A candidate needs a positive fitting gain and positive held-out gain after
Holm across both families at \Alpha. We report \OrdinaryCI\% percentile
intervals and require the step-down interval to lie above zero: the
\AdjustedCI\% interval at the first Holm step and \OrdinaryCI\% at the
second. The second cannot pass when the first fails. The README fixes the
finite-draw tail p-value and its strict threshold.

A likelihood winner also needs noninferiority of punish odds. The app's
routine review defines an error as losing at least \ErrorLoss{} points of
winning chances. A non-book next reply with an engine best move contributes
an event. The outcome is whether that best reply was played. Maia reads the
replier's converted band against the same band, as the app currently does.
The guard changes the conversion and preserves that conditioning.

Use the mean Brier score, candidate minus baseline, on verified replies
and all non-book replies, separately for each error direction. All
\GuardCells{} cells must have their \AdjustedCI\% interval upper end at
most \GuardMargin. This is a conservative correction over both candidate
guards, with all cells required within each candidate. Missing evidence or
an empty bootstrap denominator cannot pass. Brier measures calibration and
resolution together; this is not proof of calibration in every subgroup.

If both candidates qualify, the greater observed held-out gain is selected;
an exact tie selects B because it needs no owner identity. If neither
qualifies, \Base{} stays. Side and rating-third gains, first-choice shares
and probability bins are descriptive. No app port or personal fine-tuning
is part of this phase.

\section{Limits and the later port}
The predecessor explored these same games. They are held out from the new
fit, not independent of the exploration that chose the question. A fresh
archive is needed for a stronger confirmation. The bootstrap does not model
serial dependence or repeat opponents. The punish subset is small and its
verification pass selects partly on outcomes; the all-reply guard checks
that selection without removing it.

The call-site audit records the person every conversion models. A would
require both profile offsets and owner identity in review calls. B would
require a rating function and consistent inverse labels for the bot. The
punish and defense calls currently ignore the profile offset. The later
port must preserve their equal-band conditioning, refresh cached learner
levels, and distinguish the bot's explicit band from its model of the human.
The present paper supports the protocol and cost only.
