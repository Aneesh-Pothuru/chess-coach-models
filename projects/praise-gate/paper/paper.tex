\title{Does the live coach praise only the moves that earn it?\\[0.3em]

\begin{abstract}
The live coach praises two habits the moment a move lands, from the board alone: castling by
move 8, and an even trade while ahead. A change to the app holds that praise until the engine's
verdict on the move arrives, and shows it only when the verdict is a plain good one under the praise
bar of \PraiseBar{} win\%. Replayed on the player's archive at the change's own live budgets, \Moves{}
of his moves held praise. Without the gate, \FaultedAllPct\% of them would have been praised
although the archive's deeper reading says they gave away \PraiseBar{} win\% or more, and
\ClearAllPct\% although they gave away \ClearBar{} or more. With it, \FaultedPraisedPct\% of the
praised moves are faulted (95\% interval \FaultedPraisedCI) and \ClearPraised{} is clearly
faulted, while \FinePraisedPct\% of the moves the archive reads as fine keep their praise. By the
rule fixed before the run, at most \RuleFaulted{} praised moves faulted and at least \RuleFine{}
fine moves praised, the gate meets the second bar and misses the first narrowly. What it still
praises sits at the bar, where a live search and the archive's deeper one disagree as often in
one direction as the other. Read after the fact, a live bar of \CandidateBar{} win\% would sit just
inside both bars on these games; the next round tests it on games played after this run.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}

In a coached game the live coach writes notes the moment a move lands. Two of them praise a
habit: castling by move 8, and an even trade while he is ahead, which the coach says only under
the Won-Game Protocol. Those notes read only the board, so they praised a castle that passed up a
capture attacking two pieces (Figure~\ref{fig:example}) as warmly as a good one. The change measured here, at commit \texttt{\AppCommit}, holds
their praise for the engine's verdict on the move. The praise stands only when the verdict is a
plain good one (the engine's own move, or a good one that gives up less than the praise bar of
\PraiseBar{} win\%), and for a trade only when the trade still reads as a trade while ahead.

Two numbers decide whether the gate does its job: how much of the praise it lets through went to
moves that gave something away, and how much praise it withholds from moves that did not.

\section{How it was measured}

\paragraph{The moves.} The player's archive at that commit holds \Games{} games, with
\HisMoves{} moves of his. Each game is replayed from the start through the change's own instant
rules, under the coached game's policy. A move is in the study when a rule holds praise on it:
\Castles{} castles by move 8 and \Trades{} even trades while ahead, \Moves{} in all. The coach
switches the Won-Game Protocol on from its verdict on his previous move; the replay reads that
position from the archive's evaluation of it.

\paragraph{The verdict.} Each move gets the change's own verdict through the app's Node engine
service: the position before the move at the budget that names moves to him (\NamingNodes{}
nodes, \Lines{} lines), the position after it at the check budget (\CheckNodes{} nodes), one
thread, the same Stockfish build and the same budgets as on a phone or a desktop. Each move is
searched from a cleared hash, so a rerun repeats every count. A held note is praised when the
change's own test says the verdict confirms it. No move in the study had seven pieces or fewer,
so the tablebase, which the coach asks there, never speaks.

\paragraph{The reference.} The archive's own reading of the same move: the routine's sweep
searched every position at \ArchiveNodes{} nodes, which gives the win\% each move gave away on
the app's curve. A move is \emph{faulted} when it gave away \PraiseBar{} win\% or more, the praise bar,
and \emph{clearly faulted} at \ClearBar{} or more, an inaccuracy or worse on the judge's scale.

\paragraph{The rule, fixed before the first run.} The gate works when, among the moves it
praises, at most \RuleFaulted{} is faulted, and it praises at least \RuleFine{} of the moves the
archive reads as not faulted. Shares carry 95\% Wilson intervals.

\paragraph{It takes most of the faulted praise away.} The instant rules alone would have praised

\paragraph{It keeps most of the earned praise.} Of the \Fine{} moves the archive reads as fine,

\section{Where the faulted praise sits}

Every faulted move the gate praises gave away between \FaultedRangeLow{} and
\FaultedRangeHigh{} win\% by the archive's reading, the castles no more than \CastleRangeHigh,
and the archive's own classifier calls each of them good or best. All but
\FaultedPraisedOutOfLines{} of them were among the engine's \Lines{} lines in the live search, so
their loss is the gap between two lines of the naming search; the other was read by the check
after the move. The live and archive readings of the same move differ by a median of \GapMedian{} win\% and
by \GapNinety{} at the 90th percentile, and they put a move on different sides of the bar about
as often each way: \LiveOnly{} moves are faulted only by the live verdict, \ArchiveOnly{} only by
the archive. What the gate still praises is the disagreement between a \NamingNodes-node search
and a \ArchiveNodes-node one at a bar of \PraiseBar{} win\%, not moves the archive itself calls errors.

\section{What it means for the app}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The gate does what it is for on the clear cases. The instant rules alone would praise the
castle in Figure~\ref{fig:example}, which passes up a capture that attacks two pieces; the gate
withholds that praise, and of the \ClearAll{} clearly faulted moves the rules held praise on, it
praises \ClearPraised.
\item As built it misses the bar of \RuleFaulted{} narrowly, and what it lets through sits at the
praise bar, inside the noise of the live search.
\item If that bar must hold, a deeper check after the move will not reach it: all but
\FaultedPraisedOutOfLines{} of the faulted praised moves were read from the naming search's own
lines, which the check does not touch. A stricter bar for held praise, or a deeper naming search,
would.
\item A held-praise bar of \CandidateBar{} win\% would meet both bars on these games. It should be
adopted only if the next round, on games these numbers have not seen, agrees.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limits}

The reference is itself a search of bounded depth, so at the bar neither reading is the truth;
the gate is measured against the deeper one. The games are one player's, and the trades are few
enough that their row's intervals are wide. The Won-Game Protocol is read from the archive's
evaluation of his previous move, where the coach reads it from its own verdict, so a trade near
the protocol's line can fall in or out. In play the engine's hash holds the game's earlier
searches, where here each move starts from a cleared one, so a count here can move by a few in
play.

\section{The next round}

Fixed now, before any of its games are played: the candidate praises a held note only when the
gate praises it and the live loss is under \CandidateBar{} win\%. It is measured by this replay, on the games
the archive holds after \ArchiveThrough, by the same two bars; it is adopted only if it meets
both, and the gate as built is measured on the same games beside it.
