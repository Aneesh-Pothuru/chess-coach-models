\begin{abstract}
More time goes with fewer serious errors overall, but this design does not
justify changing the app's long-think triggers. The adjusted difference is
\OverallError{} percentage points. On moves made in under \PlaceboSeconds{}
seconds, where little budget effect was expected, the difference is
\PlaceboError{} points. That interval crosses the registered placebo
tolerance. The check is inconclusive and blocks every trigger decision.
After the opponent's serious error, extra time has no resolved advantage
over an ordinary move. Forcing and high danger have positive contrasts
that survive the separate Holm corrections, but the gate still leaves the
app alone. The comparison uses \PrimaryPlayers{} players in the primary
band, with at least \MinimumGames{} analysed games in each budget.
It is an observational comparison of budgets, not a causal return to
making one move take longer.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and data}
The app tells the player when to stop and think. Do its triggers mark moves
where an extra minute saves more serious errors than an extra minute at a
move with no trigger? Does another moment do better? The earlier
think-time-rapid study compared positions where players chose long or
short thinks. Here the same players chose different budgets before the
game began.

The app measured is \texttt{\AppCommit}. The data are file-order prefixes
of \Months{} from the Lichess database. The fast control is \FastControl.
Slow has a base of at least \MinBase{} seconds and base plus
\IncrementMultiplier{} times increment of at least \MinBudget{} seconds.
Lichess must label the game rated rapid in its Event header. The formula
sets a budget bar; it does not reconstruct Lichess's category.

Every move must carry a readable evaluation and clock, including a final
mate. Games end normally or on time, last at least \MinPlies{} plies, and
have ratings within \RatingGap{} points. The stream keeps only filtered
games. Names, game identifiers, player joins and move rows stay private.

\section{Measures and matching}
The reader, classifier, hanging-piece counts, move facts and triggers are
the app's, imported through the bridge. The book exit follows its archive
reader. A serious error is an app-classified mistake or blunder; there is
no miss ledger in these games. Eligible moves are graded, nontrivial and
timed, with at least \MinClock{} seconds before the move in both budgets.
The app derives a think by subtracting the clock after the move from the
previous clock for that side and adding the increment. Its flipped Black
build matched a separate Black build on \BlackChecked{} games; the build
checked \ClockChecked{} clock readings.

The triggers are book exit, conversion, forcing and structure; none means
that the app returns no trigger. Candidates are the move after the
opponent's serious error, being in check, following a capture, a hanging
piece on either side, defending at \DefendingCp{} centipawns or worse,
the player's book exit and next \EarlyMoves{} moves, and high danger.
Candidates overlap. High danger starts at the \HighQuantile{}th percentile
on primary quick moves pooled across budgets. Quick means under the app's
\QuickSeconds{}-second line.

The danger model is a fixed LightGBM classifier with \TreeCount{} trees
and \FoldCount{} player folds. It learns from fast-budget quick moves.
For a held-out player fold, training excludes every game involving any
player in that fold, including the opponent's side. Inputs describe the
board and ratings before the move. Raw clocks, opponent think and every
time-control field are omitted. The danger model stays fixed during the
player bootstrap; it is not refitted within a draw.

For each moment T, \StrataCount{} danger strata are cut on its primary
fast moves, using all think lengths. Within a player, a stratum contributes
only when both budgets have T moves there. The slow-minus-fast differences
in error rate and think time receive weights proportional to that player's
fast moves in contributing strata. Players then receive equal weight.
Their averages are $\Delta\mathrm{err}_T$ and
$\Delta\mathrm{think}_T$. The saving per extra minute is
\[
v_T=-\SecondsPerMinute\,
\frac{\Delta\mathrm{err}_T}{\Delta\mathrm{think}_T}.
\]
This is a ratio of average differences. It is not an average of individual
players' ratios.

T and none are compared on the same players with common strata in both
sets. Each comparison therefore has its own paired none estimate. Coverage
is the equal-player mean fraction of fast moves retained from the players
with T and none moves in both budgets before matching. Players lost in
matching count as zero. The all-move and placebo checks use the same
estimator without pairing to none.

\section{The fixed rule}
The complete rule was committed before this effect pass. Its original
README, paper and generator remain archived byte for byte, and the
original content manifest still verifies the rule, analysis code,
dependencies and input reports. The publication changed the answer and
paper, not the estimator or decision thresholds.

Intervals use \Draws{} Poisson bootstrap draws over players, seed \Seed.
Every comparison uses the same draws. Models, strata and support remain
fixed. Intervals are the \Confidence\% percentile intervals. A comparison
needs \MinPlayers{} matched players and \Coverage\% fast-move coverage at
both T and none. For each ratio, the lower endpoint for extra think must
be at least \ThinkFloor{} seconds and every bootstrap denominator must be
positive.

The overall error interval must be wholly below zero. For eligible moves
under \PlaceboSeconds{} seconds in both budgets, the entire error interval
must lie strictly inside plus or minus \PlaceboPoints{} percentage point.
A failed or inconclusive gate blocks changes. Given both gates, support
and extra think, a trigger is kept when its error interval is below zero
and its value exceeds paired none with Holm-adjusted p below \Alpha.
A significant disadvantage removes its special priority. The correction
covers all \TriggerCount{} triggers. The \CandidateCount{} candidates
form a separate family; a candidate joins only when it cuts errors and
exceeds none under those same interval and corrected-comparison conditions.
Ineligible comparisons enter their family with unit p-value.

\section{After the opponent's serious error first}
This candidate was the leading question before the effect pass. In its
paired cohort, the slow budget adds \AfterThink{} seconds of think. Its
error difference is \AfterError{} percentage points. The saving per extra
minute is \AfterValue{} serious errors; its advantage over paired none is
\AfterContrast, with Holm p = \AfterP. Both the error and advantage
intervals cross zero. This is not evidence for adding a special long-think
instruction there, even apart from the placebo gate.

\section{Limits and the app}
The budget is chosen before the position, but not assigned at random.
The same player may choose it when more rested, more serious or facing a
different opponent. Repeated players remove fixed differences between
people, not those changing circumstances. Analysed games are selected
games, and file prefixes need not represent later days.

Both opponents receive the larger budget. Boards, opponent errors and
survival above the clock floor can already reflect it. Matching observed
board difficulty conditions on part of that change. The app's check-giving
and pawn-capture triggers also depend partly on the move played, which
more time can change. The ratio is an association with the budget, not a
causal return to forcing a particular move to take another minute.

A player bootstrap does not capture every dependence between opponents.
Its intervals also condition on the fitted danger model. Whole-second
clocks limit precision, and slow mixes controls and increments. The
placebo can expose a problem but cannot prove the absence of confounding.
Here it did not establish that the design is trustworthy enough for the
registered app decision.

The triggers stay as they are. No candidate is added and no trigger loses
priority. The \DeepSeconds{}-second and \BookSeconds{}-second bars are
unchanged: comparing budgets does not identify those move-level bars.
The positive forcing and high-danger contrasts, and the unresolved move
after the opponent's error, remain descriptions for a later design to
test.
