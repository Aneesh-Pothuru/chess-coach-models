\begin{abstract}
The earlier think-time study compared positions where players chose long
and short thinks. This design compares the same players after they chose
different time controls. The fast budget is \FastControl; the slow budget
has a base of at least \MinBase{} seconds and base plus \IncrementMultiplier{}
times increment of at least \MinBudget{} seconds, in games Lichess labels
rapid. The sampled prefixes contain \PrimaryPlayers{} players in the primary
rating band with at least \MinimumGames{} analysed games in each budget.
The app's own instruments built \BuiltGames{} games for selected players.
This paper reports counts and timings. No budget effect, error rate,
per-moment think mean, interval or trigger verdict has been computed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and data}
Does an extra minute spent at one of the app's long-think triggers save
more serious errors than a minute at an ordinary move? Does another moment
do better? The triggers come from \texttt{triggerOf} at app commit
\texttt{\AppCommit}. The data are file-order prefixes of \Months{} from the
Lichess database. Names, games and player joins stay private. Every move
must carry a readable evaluation and clock. Games end normally or on time,
have at least \MinPlies{} plies, and ratings within \RatingGap{} points.
Unlike the predecessor, a final mate is not exempt from the evaluation
requirement.

\section{Measures and matching}
The reader, classifier, hanging-piece counts, move facts and triggers are
the app's, imported through the bridge. The book exit and absence of a miss
ledger follow the predecessor. A serious error is an app-classified
mistake or blunder. Eligible moves are graded, nontrivial and timed, with
at least \MinClock{} seconds available before the move in either budget.
The app already adds the increment when deriving a think. Its flipped
Black build matched a separate Black build on \BlackChecked{} games.

The nuisance danger model uses \FoldCount{} player folds. A training fold
excludes both sides of every game involving a held-out player. It learns
from fast-budget quick moves and omits clocks, opponent think and every
time-control field. The fixed fit is used here only to count high-danger
moves and common strata. No performance or error-rate diagnostic is read.

For each moment, danger quintiles of its primary fast moves define the
strata. Within a player, only strata containing moves in both budgets
contribute. Their weights are the player's fast move counts. Players then
receive equal weight. Both the error difference and think difference use
the same weights. Their ratio is errors saved per extra minute. A moment
and ordinary moves are compared only on the intersection of their
supported players.

\section{The fixed rule}
The README gives the complete specification. There are \Draws{} Poisson
bootstrap draws over players, seed \Seed, shared by every comparison.
Scores, strata and support stay fixed. A comparison needs \MinPlayers{}
matched players and \Coverage\% of its equal-player fast-move weight.
Both think differences must have a \Confidence\% lower interval endpoint
of at least \ThinkFloor{} seconds, with every bootstrap denominator positive.
Otherwise the ratio is not estimated.

The slow budget must reduce errors over all eligible moves, with the
interval wholly below zero. For moves under \PlaceboSeconds{} seconds in
both budgets, the entire interval must lie inside plus or minus
\PlaceboPoints{} percentage point. A failed or inconclusive check blocks
trigger changes. Given those checks, a trigger is kept when extra time
cuts its error rate and its saving per minute exceeds ordinary moves
with Holm-adjusted p below \Alpha. A significant disadvantage removes its
special priority. The correction covers all \TriggerCount{} triggers.
The \CandidateCount{} candidates form a separate Holm family; a candidate
joins only when it cuts errors and exceeds ordinary moves. Otherwise the
app stays as it is. This does not recalibrate its \DeepSeconds{}-second
or \BookSeconds{}-second bars.

\section{Limits and the app}
The budget was chosen before the position, but not at random. A player may
choose more time when rested or more serious. Both opponents receive the
larger budget. The board, opponent's errors and survival above the minimum
clock can already reflect it. Matching those features conditions on part
of the budget's effect. The ratio is an association with the budget, not
a causal return to forcing a particular move to take another minute.

Analysed games are selected games; file prefixes need not represent later
days. A player bootstrap does not capture all dependence between opponents.
Whole-second clocks limit precision. Slow pools the time controls listed
in the count report. None of these counts justifies changing the app.
The later effect pass will apply the committed rule without changing it.
